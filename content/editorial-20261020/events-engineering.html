Represent event feasibility before promising a package

A catering enquiry needs a date, approximate guest count, venue area and service format before a team can judge fit. A drop-off order, staffed buffet and multi-day event require different resources. Use a structured brief with conditional questions, but keep dietary and accessibility needs as information to review rather than an automatic promise that every requirement can be fulfilled.

For planners, a venue shortlist should identify actual constraints such as permitted setup hours and whether the listed capacity is seated or standing. Confirm these facts with the operator before publication. A venue photograph does not establish permission to use the property on the customer's preferred date. Clearly mark an enquiry as awaiting availability and scope confirmation.

Design date handling around the event, not the browser

Store an event date and local event timezone deliberately rather than converting a date-only selection through an arbitrary browser timezone. A midnight conversion can otherwise move the displayed date for someone organising an event from abroad. Test date-only values separately from arrival and service times. For multi-day work, show start and end dates in the acknowledgement so the organiser can catch a misunderstanding early.

If packages include calculated estimates, calculate the authoritative amount on the server from the current package version and clearly identify excluded or variable items. A browser estimate should never become an unreviewed commitment for staffing, transport or equipment. The first website release may reasonably use enquiry-only pricing until the business has reliable rules for those variables.

Keep menus searchable and sample events honest

Publish menus in accessible text with categories and maintained descriptions. A downloadable brochure can supplement the page, but an image-only menu makes mobile comparison difficult. Distinguish a sample menu from a confirmed offering for a specific event, and check dietary descriptions with the business rather than infer them from a dish name. Ingredient or allergy questions need a human confirmation process.

Event stories should identify the organiser's actual role. Catering, decor and complete event management are not interchangeable contributions. Use photographs with permission and avoid publishing guest lists, private addresses or identifiable children without the appropriate review. A hypothetical planning example can explain a workflow, but it must not be presented as an event the company delivered.

Rehearse the operational handoff

Send a test enquiry for a busy date, resubmit it after a simulated connection failure and ask a staff member to identify the original request. Confirm that the business can update availability notes without editing the whole website. The acknowledgement should say who will assess the brief and what information may be requested next, without claiming that a calendar entry or WhatsApp click has secured the event.

A useful event website reduces ambiguous conversations: the customer understands the proposed service and the operator receives enough context to evaluate it. That is a concrete acceptance criterion, whereas a repeated promise of more leads tells neither party whether the implementation works.
