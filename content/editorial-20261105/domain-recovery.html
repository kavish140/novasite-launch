A website outage, an expired domain and a failed email subscription are different incidents. They may happen together, but recovering one does not necessarily recover the others. Before changing anything, identify which service failed and who controls the account needed to restore it.

This guide is a recovery checklist, not a report of a specific SiteNova client outage. Domain registration, DNS, website hosting and email can be supplied by different organisations under separate renewal agreements.

Identify the failed service before paying a renewal

Check the domain's recorded registration state through the registrar and the business's account. Separately inspect DNS, hosting billing and the email provider's status. A hosting suspension can leave domain registration intact. A DNS change can interrupt mail while a website still loads from another configuration. A cached page on one device does not establish that other visitors can reach it.

Record the observed error, affected hostname and time. Check the service provider's own status and account notices. Do not assume that an unexpected parking page is the original website or enter credentials into a recovery link supplied by an unknown message.

Check the actual domain recovery terms

Renewal, expiration, redemption and deletion procedures depend on the domain and provider. Ask the sponsoring registrar which state the registration is in, who can authorise recovery, which deadline applies and what the actual charge will be. Avoid relying on a generic number of days or a claimed multiple of the annual price.

ICANN's generic top-level-domain lifecycle guidance explains the distinctions between lifecycle stages. It should not be applied as an identical timetable for every country-code domain or provider arrangement. Act promptly, verify the applicable terms and retain the support reference.

If a previous developer registered the domain, establish whether the business is the registrant and what documentation the registrar needs. Public registration information does not grant control of the account. Recover access through the provider's process rather than guessing credentials or commissioning a replacement domain before assessing the existing one.

Preserve the DNS configuration

Before replacing nameservers or restoring records, obtain the available configuration and identify which services each record supports. Website addresses, mail delivery and verification records can have different destinations. Restoring only the website record may leave mail broken; copying an old configuration without checking changes can introduce another outage.

Record which values were changed and when. Allow for the actual caching behaviour when checking recovery from different networks. Do not promise simultaneous failure or restoration for every device and service. Where the original configuration is uncertain, consult the relevant provider before deleting records.

Verify that a hosting backup is usable

Ask what the provider retains and whether those files or records are available in the current account state. A statement that backups exist is not a restoration test. Check the date, coverage and access needed to restore the application, uploaded files and database. Keep sensitive recovery material private.

Restore into a controlled environment where practical, and test the important visitor tasks before switching production traffic. For a website with bookings or payment records, avoid replacing current transactional data with an older snapshot without a reviewed recovery plan. A restored homepage does not establish that all integrations or private records are correct.

Evaluate search effects from the actual response

Inspect the HTTP responses Google and visitors can receive during the incident. An error response, a redirect and a parked page returning success are different situations. Google's HTTP and network-error guidance describes how response types can affect crawling and indexing. The duration and observed behaviour matter; this guide cannot predict a fixed ranking loss or recovery time for an unknown site.

After restoring service, check important routes, redirects, robots directives and the sitemap. Use available Search Console reports to distinguish an indexing problem from a change in demand or measurement. A domain coming back online does not establish that every old URL or external integration has been restored.

Make renewal responsibility explicit

Keep a private inventory of provider, business account owner, renewal date, billing route and escalation contact. Use reminders that reach someone who still works with the business, and keep recovery access current. Auto-renewal is useful only if its payment method and account access remain valid.

Agree which checks are included in maintenance and who performs them. Review access recovery and backup restoration before an emergency. The objective is a recoverable operating arrangement with known owners, rather than a promise that one subscription prevents every outage.
