Before buying an integration described as AI shopping, ask what the system can actually do for a particular merchant today. Finding a product, preparing a basket, authorising a payment and confirming an order are separate capabilities. An announcement about one does not establish the others.

This is a merchant planning guide, not a claim that every AI assistant can purchase from every Indian website. It removes the earlier unsupported conclusion that offline shops inevitably lose their customers unless they commission an e-commerce store.

Check the payment product, not just the announcement

Ask the provider for its current documentation, merchant eligibility, supported bank or app, customer-authorisation flow and availability in the relevant environment. Distinguish an announced concept, an invite-only pilot, a sandbox integration and a production capability enabled for your account. Write down the evidence and its date before making a customer promise.

NPCI's 8 October 2025 Reserve Pay circular describes reserving funds for multiple customer-initiated debits. That document alone does not establish a general permission for a shopping agent to spend from any user's account. Product-specific authorisation, limits and merchant support require their own verification.

Likewise, the UPI HELP Assistant pilot circular concerns payment support. A support assistant is not evidence of an autonomous checkout service. This review did not verify the original article's Unified Agent Protocol claim or a universal AI-agent spending allowance, so neither is treated here as an available merchant requirement.

Decide whether the sale is ready for checkout

Consider an illustrative wholesaler selling branded notebooks. A buyer requests two hundred units with a printed logo, but artwork approval, available paper stock, tax treatment, freight and the required dispatch date remain unresolved. Sending that request straight to payment can create a commitment the business cannot fulfil. A structured quotation can be the more appropriate next step.

A repeat retail purchase with a confirmed SKU, current stock, fixed amount and known delivery address has different requirements. Identify who can confirm each input and how often it changes. An owned store, a marketplace or an assisted ordering process may each fit parts of the workflow. Compare the operating requirements before selecting a platform.

Give products identities that survive a conversation

Assign a stable SKU and distinguish variants such as pack size, colour and material. Keep public descriptions aligned with the source used to quote or fulfil an order. If the website says a product is available but staff must still check the shelf, describe availability as subject to confirmation rather than presenting an estimate as live stock.

Structured data can describe visible product facts, but it is not an inventory reservation system or a guarantee of discovery. Google's guidance for AI search features does not require a special AI schema. Search inclusion, assistant recommendations and payment-provider compatibility are different questions; validate each through the actual integration.

Keep intent, authorisation and settlement separate

For any future provider-supported assistant integration, define the boundary of permission: who is acting, which merchant receives payment, what amount is approved and how permission can be withdrawn. Do not infer consent from a chat message or from a browser opening a payment page. The provider's supported authorisation process must govern the transaction.

The merchant backend should bind an order to its actual items, amount and currency, then verify provider-confirmed payment state before showing paid status. A return URL, an assistant's summary or a screenshot is not settlement evidence. Duplicate notifications and interrupted browser sessions should be tested without creating duplicate fulfilment.

Plan the exception path too. Staff need to distinguish an unconfirmed request, a failed attempt, a pending verification, a captured payment and a refund under review. An automated purchase still needs support when a product changes or delivery cannot be met. Provider records and the merchant's order records must be reconcilable.

Test one complete order before expanding

  1. Choose a small set of products with approved descriptions and an accountable stock source.
  2. Run discovery, selection and quantity validation through the proposed supported interface.
  3. Verify the exact authorised payment and the server's order state in the provider's test environment.
  4. Exercise changed stock, a different total, interrupted checkout and repeated callbacks.
  5. Record which steps still require a person and how the customer reaches that person.

A CSV import is only a snapshot unless a separate synchronisation process is implemented. If the business uses a billing or stock system, establish its supported interface, update frequency, conflict rules and failure recovery. Do not sell a one-time file import as automatic two-way synchronisation.

Compare total operating cost without invented margins

Ask for the actual payment, marketplace, hosting, maintenance, tax and fulfilment costs for the selected arrangement. An independent store does not retain one hundred percent of gross revenue after those costs. A marketplace's fee also cannot be represented by one universal percentage across categories and agreements.

Start with the current bottleneck. If buyers struggle to identify products but every order needs negotiation, a searchable enquiry catalogue can be considered before automated checkout. Where standard orders are ready for payment, compare their fulfilment and recovery requirements using the store scope guide.
