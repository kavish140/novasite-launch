A returned cash-on-delivery parcel creates several separate accounting entries: forward freight, any reverse charge, packaging, payment or handling charges and inventory unavailable during transit. An order counter hides those differences. Before changing checkout, reconcile delivered and returned orders against the courier invoice and identify which losses the business can actually measure.

This guide uses illustrative calculations and implementation choices, not a measured SiteNova merchant result. It does not establish a national COD return rate, a universal prepaid return rate or a guaranteed improvement from a discount. Prices, delivery conditions and refund obligations must come from the merchant's actual provider agreements and reviewed policies.

Calculate the cost of one returned shipment

Suppose an example order incurs ₹120 forward freight and ₹110 reverse freight. Those two charges total ₹230. Packaging, acquisition cost, recoverable inventory and any provider adjustments need separate treatment. Do not automatically count the product's full cost as a loss if the item returns in saleable condition, or assume every parcel has the same courier tariff.

Build a simple reconciliation sheet using the order reference, dispatch date, final delivery state and invoiced charges. Separate cancellations before dispatch from returns after an attempt. Check whether the carrier has invoiced a charge provisionally or finally. The useful comparison is the merchant's own delivered-order margin and exception cost over a defined period.

Distinguish incorrect information from an unwilling buyer

A failed delivery can follow an incomplete address, an unavailable recipient, a serviceability problem, an unexpected amount or another operational issue. Do not assign a motive such as prank buying from a courier status alone. Staff may need to verify the reason with the carrier and customer before changing the order's state.

Collect address fields appropriate to the delivery service and allow the customer to review them. Show the final payable amount and any delivery conditions before submission. An address check can catch missing information without proving that the recipient will accept the parcel. Keep a correction history when staff change a delivery instruction, and avoid exposing a customer's address in analytics or shared public links.

Offer prepaid payment without promising zero return risk

A merchant can evaluate a disclosed prepaid incentive against its margin and provider costs. State the offer amount, eligibility, duration and treatment of cancellation or refund. A prepaid order can still be returned, disputed or impossible to deliver. It is not a guaranteed sale merely because a payment has been captured.

For an illustrative ₹50 discount, compare the resulting delivered margin with the observed difference in exception costs. Use comparable products and periods where possible. A change in courier, campaign audience or dispatch time can affect the result too. A small sample may justify another test without supporting a broad claim that payment method caused the entire difference.

Make any COD fee and confirmation step explicit

If the business proposes a handling fee, review its presentation and cancellation/refund treatment against applicable obligations and the actual agreement. Do not label a fee non-refundable by default. Show it before the customer commits, include it in the authoritative server calculation and keep it consistent across the cart, confirmation and invoice.

Verification can establish that someone controls a contact channel, but an OTP does not prove delivery intent or identity beyond that limited check. Select an appropriate provider, rate-limit attempts and protect verification secrets. Explain the purpose of the message and respect messaging permissions. Do not invent a universal OTP length, claim verification eliminates false orders or silently add marketing consent.

Keep payment recovery separate from dispatch

An order may be awaiting payment, paid, held for review, dispatched or cancelled. Model those states explicitly. A successful browser callback is not enough to authorise fulfilment: verify payment through the provider and reconcile the recorded amount and order relationship. A delayed result should recover the original order rather than invite another payment immediately.

If a COD buyer later pays through a link, the backend must prevent two collection paths from remaining active for the same obligation. Confirm the payment before instructing the carrier or fulfilment team to change collection. Handle a parcel already handed over as a reviewed exception. The website should explain the current state without promising that a payment click instantly changes a courier's instruction.

Route uncertain orders to a bounded human review

The business may decide that an unusual quantity, unserviceable destination or conflicting customer instruction requires review. Define those rules using evidence and an escalation owner. Avoid treating an arbitrary order-value threshold as a universal fraud detector. A hold should have a clear reason and next step, rather than leave a customer waiting indefinitely.

Give staff the information needed to assess the order without unrestricted access to every customer's history. Record who approved dispatch or cancellation. Repeated messages and duplicate webhooks must not create duplicate shipments. Test a network interruption after order acceptance and confirm that a retry returns the existing reference.

Measure delivery outcomes after the test window closes

Choose a cohort with enough time for dispatch and delivery exceptions to resolve. Recently placed orders should not be compared with a mature cohort as though every pending parcel succeeded. Report the number of delivered, returned, cancelled and unresolved orders with the denominator used for each percentage.

Review the operational result before expanding a checkout change. A lower recorded return percentage is not useful if successful orders also fell sharply or charges were omitted. Retain the assumptions, provider terms and observed limitations in the decision record. A reliable ordering process improves clarity and control; the merchant's own reconciled data determines whether a particular intervention improved its economics.
